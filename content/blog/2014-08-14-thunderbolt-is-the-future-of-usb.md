AT A GLANCE Today we are surrounded by many different electronic devices and each one of them requires at least one connector and for most of them the connector isn’t the same.

Apple’s smartphones uses Lightning cables, Samsung tablets uses mini USB cables, external Hard drive uses micro USB cables and so on.

If you have a desk with at least a laptop and two or more external devices you also have a lot of cables that are really living around your desk and, if you are a simplicity lover like me, also you hate that kind of mess.

Sometimes happen that you take a cable, you quickly check it twice and themself (it’s absolutely normal if you are a nerd-geek soul): for what I use it?

USB2, USB3, firewire, power supply cables, mouse cables, keyboard cables, webcam cables, HDMI cables … is like a technology net that surround our desk every day.

UNDERSTAND THE DIFFERENCES Understanding the differences between every single cables and connectors isn’t simple: there are power connectors that must be thick because they must bear high current and heat; there are data cables that are used to transmit information between a device to another one and there are the ethernet cables that are, in my opinion, the most ugliest of all.

Every single connector is studied to improve it’s performance and to supply to a particular work that could be, as I said before, carry data or power energy.

Unfortunately not all the cables are identical and, most important thing, not all the connector are similar.

There are particular kind of connectors that have a microchip inside himself like lightning connectors and thunderbolt as well, instead USB cables are simply “ wire and plastic ” cables without any kind of intelligence.

But why we need all this cables?

What is the best between **Thunderbolt** and USB 3?

THUNDERBOLT Perhaps the thunderbolt connector isn’t the most widespread interface but it has the most interesting features of all the other connector.

The TB connector has the same design of the Mini Display Port interface (more info here) and it gets the same advantages like: small connector and simple design.

The **Thunderbolt** interface was co-developed by Apple and Intel and it was commercially introduced in 2011.

A single **Thunderbolt** port can supports up to six **Thunderbolt** devices (for example, the new Mac Pro has 6 **Thunderbolt** ports for a total of 36 external devices).

Another important features is that the **Thunderbolt** gives two channel of communication so it can carry up to 20 Gb/s (10+10) at the same time.

This huge bandwidth allow to manage a 4K display without any kind of problem and allow to transfer a huge quantity of data: it’s a very helpful and interesting thing if you work with video or music.

Another great advantage is that the **Thunderbolt** port is exactly the half width of the USB port, that allow to reduce the size of our electronic devices where the thunderbolt replaces the USB.

USB 3 OR THUNDERBOLT Right now the USB 3 (and 2.0) is the most widespread connector in all the different electronic devices from the laptop to the smartphone.

The USB 3 is cheaper, best known and less new than the thunderbolt and all these facts are very important when it comes to deciding what will be the communication interface to be used in future.

The **Thunderbolt** is faster but this maybe is something that people doesn’t care about; they want a common and simple-to-use interface that can be connected in almost all computer without using adapters or strange external hubs.

The thunderbolt is faster, smaller and (maybe) too futuristic for the “common” people but if you are looking for the faster, smaller and clever interface ever perhaps you have found it.
